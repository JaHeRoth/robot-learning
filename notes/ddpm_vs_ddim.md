# How DDPM and DDIM do inference

A perfect sampler could produce x₀ ~ p_data by drawing xₙ from the fully noised marginal q(xₙ), then drawing xₙ₋₁ ~ q(xₙ₋₁|xₙ), then xₙ₋₂ ~ q(xₙ₋₂|xₙ₋₁), and so on, since by induction every xⱼ (and finally x₀) then follows the right distribution. DDPM implements this with two substitutions: N(0, I) in place of q(xₙ) (exact when ᾱₙ ≈ 0, where ᾱⱼ = ∏(1−βᵢ) is the fraction of signal variance surviving at rung j), and a Gaussian with network-supplied mean in place of each q(xⱼ|xⱼ₊₁). The problem is that q(xⱼ|xⱼ₊₁) isn't Gaussian in general. It would be if x₀ were pinned: q(xⱼ|xⱼ₊₁, x₀) has a Gaussian prior q(xⱼ|x₀) and a Gaussian likelihood q(xⱼ₊₁|xⱼ) whose mean is linear in xⱼ (the conjugate Normal-Normal setup). Without x₀ pinned, the prior becomes the marginal of xⱼ, a mixture over possible x₀, so q(xⱼ|xⱼ₊₁) becomes a mixture of bridges, one per plausible x₀: non-Gaussian in general, and multimodal when the data's modes are far apart at that noise level. What saves DDPM is that the bridge's dependence on x₀ scales with βⱼ₊₁/(1−ᾱⱼ₊₁) (the fresh noise relative to the noise already accumulated), so away from the first few rungs, small steps make the bridges' separation (order β) vanish against their width (order √β), leaving q(xⱼ|xⱼ₊₁) approximately Gaussian. If we instead jump several rungs at once, the effective β is everything accumulated across the skipped rungs, the bridges separate, and we end up sampling a single Gaussian where the true conditional is multimodal, an error that compounds down the chain.

Nothing forces inference to sample its way down the chain step by step, though. The one hard requirement is that each xⱼ the sampler visits must be in-distribution for that rung's training inputs, since the network's estimates only mean anything on inputs like the ones it trained on. DDPM imagines the noising chain as xⱼ := √(1−βⱼ)xⱼ₋₁ + √βⱼ εⱼ, with fresh noise at every step. DDIM instead imagines that the noise was drawn once per trajectory: draw ε ~ N(0, I) once, then set xⱼ := √ᾱⱼx₀ + √(1−ᾱⱼ)ε for every j. This is exactly the noising formula used during training, so the imagined chain reproduces not just every marginal q(xⱼ) but every conditional q(xⱼ|x₀), i.e. the joint the network was trained on, and thus the same target E[ε|xⱼ].

DDIM then produces an x₀ by drawing xₙ ~ N(0, I) and following the trajectory √ᾱⱼx̂₀ + √(1−ᾱⱼ)ε̂ from j = n down to 0, where ε̂ is the network output at the current stop and x̂₀ follows from inverting the blend, i.e. a running estimate of which trajectory we're on, revised at every stop. Since nothing is sampled along the way, no multimodal conditional ever gets replaced by a Gaussian draw, and what's left to get right is the endpoint: over random xₙ, the map from xₙ to x₀ must land on p_data. Errors come from the estimate itself and from the coarseness of the stops, since the estimated trajectory bends as it gets revised and few stops cut those corners (this also lets the iterates drift away from the distribution the network was trained on), plus the same ᾱₙ ≈ 0 approximation as before. DDPM's step over the same gap has a second job on top of aiming: it must also draw the spread of the true conditional, and a Gaussian can't represent that spread once the gap is large. So a large gap costs DDIM some curvature error, whereas DDPM pays in wrongly-shaped randomness that never gets removed. That's why we can denoise in ~10 DDIM steps but need way more DDPM steps.

A compact way to summarize the difference: during inference both methods need each visited marginal p(xⱼ) to match the training-time q(xⱼ), partly to keep the network on-distribution at every stop, and ultimately so that the endpoint lands on p(x₀) ≈ p_data, which is the actual prize. Matching every one-step conditional, p(xⱼ|xⱼ₊₁) ≈ q(xⱼ|xⱼ₊₁), is sufficient for that but not necessary: many update rules transport q(xⱼ₊₁) onto q(xⱼ), and the true reverse conditional is just one of them. DDPM commits to that one, and inherits its price of approximate Gaussianity only at small steps. DDIM picks a different member of the family, a deterministic map whose induced conditional is a point mass, nothing like q(xⱼ|xⱼ₊₁), yet which pushes the marginals forward correctly anyway. Each choice carries its own discretization tax (the spread-drawing vs the curvature error from before). In the continuous-time limit this same fact is the known equivalence between the diffusion SDE and its probability flow ODE: identical marginals, different process. The marginals underdetermine the process, and DDIM lives in that slack.
